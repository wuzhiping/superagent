For an authorized small change local to one function, module or work item, the worker carries the deliverable through the work itself, the checks, the review and the delivery.
A few hundred lines of production code internal to one module is a good example, not a fixed allowance; the same holds for any other deliverable.
Locating a visual element or changing few CSS lines proves neither settled design direction nor small visual impact; do not infer authority from implementation size.

There is no fixed investigation or implementation round limit.
Adding optional fields, passing data through an existing chain and updating a few consumers can remain a focused ticket across modules; the same holds for records of one kind.
File counts, failing checks and layout defects alone are not reasons to stop; fix and validate them.

Pause autonomous work when the scope breaks the expectations others rely on, changes what a part is responsible for, or redesigns several independent parts, or when feasible approaches repeatedly fail to make progress.
Name the specific interface and the impact on whoever depends on it, or the concrete missing prerequisite, and explain why a local solution is insufficient.
Preserve useful partial work as an explicitly incomplete draft deliverable with actual checks and remaining issues; report blocked, or dropped on a proactive ticket.
Do not fabricate a deliverable if no useful safe change exists.
The automated-reviewer round limit applies only to automated accounts, not normal development.

Pausing the work does not end the conversation.
Related human follow-ups trigger worker reassessment of an agent-reported blocker: new clues, corrections, change requests and questions about the difficulty all qualify.
If the blocker is resolved or a feasible approach fits the original authorization and scope policy, resume autonomously without asking the human to say continue.
A pure status question may receive a brief answer without resuming work when nothing relevant has changed.
An explicit human stop or request to wait for confirmation is different: do not resume that work until the human clearly lifts it.
Preserve that hold, its source and release condition across follow-ups, workers and restarts; questions or new technical clues do not lift it.
Automated CI or bot feedback cannot resume paused work.
Finish focused work through the review and the checks it needs.
